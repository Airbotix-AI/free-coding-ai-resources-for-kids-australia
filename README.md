# Free Coding and AI Learning Resources for Kids in Australia

**18 curated resources**, with official links, age guidance, a useful learning outcome and the actual free-access conditions. Checked **19 October 2026 (Australia/Brisbane)**. Start with a finished idea: make a story, build a game, or explain and test an AI prediction.

Maintained by [Airbotix](https://airbotix.ai/), an Australian education provider. This is a free reference: no subscription, paid API or Airbotix booking is required. Third-party tools retain their own terms. Free access is not the same as open-source licensing.

[Learning paths](LEARNING-PATHS.md) · [Worked examples](WORKED-EXAMPLES.md) · [Free scope and parent setup](FREE-SCOPE.md) · [Corresponding articles and repositories](RESOURCES.md) · [Source audit](SOURCES.md)

## Choose a first result

| Child or situation | Start here | A visible result |
|---|---|---|
| Ages 5–7, with an adult | ScratchJr or CS Unplugged | A short story or a paper instruction sequence |
| Ages 8–11, first independent project | Scratch, Code Club Australia or MakeCode Arcade | A game someone else can play |
| Ages 12–16, ready to read and debug | Grok Academy or Raspberry Pi projects | A Python/web project with tests |
| Learning what AI does and gets wrong | Day of AI or Experience AI | A checked explanation and model-evaluation record |
| No accounts or extra hardware wanted | CS Unplugged, Blockly Games or MakeCode simulator | A solved puzzle or simulated interactive project |

These pathways are editorial suggestions, not provider admission rules. In the table, **provider** marks an explicitly published range; **editorial** marks our suggested starting fit. Digital-safety/teacher resources are labelled separately from child-facing courses.

| [Grok Academy](https://groklearning.com/) · [details](resources/coding.md#grok) | Primary–secondary (course-specific) | A Python or Blockly solution with tested cases | All content free for Australian and Aotearoa NZ students per provider policy. Browser JavaScript and optional camera/mic; saving/export choices differ. |
| [AI for Oceans — CodeAI](https://studio.code.org/s/oceans) · [details](resources/ai.md#ai-oceans) | 8–14 (editorial) | An explanation of how missing training examples change predictions | Free provider-hosted learning activity. | [CS Unplugged](https://www.csunplugged.org/en/) · [details](resources/support.md#cs-unplugged) | 5–14 (editorial; choose by activity) | A paper algorithm, sorting trace or binary message | Free teaching materials; ordinary craft/printing costs may apply. Public reading; no child account needed for paper activities. |
| [Australian Digital Technologies Hub](https://www.digitaltechnologieshub.edu.au/) · [details](resources/support.md#dt-hub) | Foundation–Year 10 families/educators | A selected activity and a short explanation of the concept | Public Hub guides and resources; linked third-party services may charge. Hub reading is public; external resource accounts and prices vary. |
| [Computer Science Field Guide](https://www.csfieldguide.org.nz/en/) · [details](resources/support.md#cs-field) | 12–16 (editorial) | An explanation supported by an interactive algorithm example | Free online textbook and interactive resources. | [eSafety Kids](https://www.esafety.gov.au/kids) · [details](resources/support.md#esafety) | Primary-age child with adult (editorial) | A practical help-seeking and personal-information plan | Public government guidance. Public reading; no account needed to read the listed guidance. |

Teachable Machine is an **access-limited** entry: its production text did not fully render during our check. It is not one of the quick-start paths; confirm it works before choosing it. No resource here was tested through a complete signed-in course, child account, upload or training session. See the per-resource source notes.
